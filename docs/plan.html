You told me the only thing you have ever sustained is work: waking up and turning up. That is not a small admission, it is the design key. Everything below is built out of it.

The one thing that has stuck

“The biggest thing I've sustained is consistency with work. Waking up and turning up. Everything else has faded away.”

Two years of it. Through a house move, a country move, a seven-hour time shift and a shift pattern that would break most people. So the honest reading is not that you cannot sustain things. You sustain one very specific kind of thing, reliably, at a level most people never manage.

The rule everything follows from

Anything that looks like a job will stick. Anything that looks like a personal goal will fade. You have two years of evidence for the first half and a lifetime for the second.

So stop trying to become a person with better habits. Give the things you want the properties of work instead: a time you did not choose, a person expecting you, a role with your name on it, and no daily decision to make.

Before anything else: check your pass

You said that if NEXT says no, the fallback is Strait Up Growth — finding clients who pay you for your services, to stay in Singapore and keep your Employment Pass. That plan has a gate in front of it that is worth knowing about before October rather than after.

What MOM actually says

An Employment Pass is tied to one sponsoring employer. MOM's own guidance is that work pass holders must only work for their designated employer and must not take on additional jobs or engage in activities to earn additional income in Singapore.

Taking client fees for consulting work alongside an EP job is secondary employment. It needs MOM's prior consent or a separate valid work pass — including if you do it at weekends, and including if the client is overseas but you are doing the work here.

Owning shares is fine. Being a passive shareholder is not working. The line is between owning and doing.

So the question that decides your Plan B is: who sponsors your pass? I do not know, and you should not guess either. The three cases are genuinely different.

If NEXT
Then Strait Up Growth cannot take paid client work while that pass is live, without MOM consent. And if you leave NEXT, the pass goes with the job. October is not just about your evenings. It is about your immigration status. Which is exactly why it should not be framed as an ultimatum.
If Strait Up Growth
Then you are already on the own-company route and the constraint is different: the company needs to keep paying you a qualifying fixed monthly salary and show commercial justification at renewal. Client revenue is not a nice-to-have, it is what renews the pass. That makes the morning block below urgent rather than optional.
If a Dependant's Pass
Then note that the Letter of Consent route for DP holders was largely closed in May 2021. DP business owners can still qualify in some cases, but the criteria are strict — including employing at least one Singaporean or PR at the Local Qualifying Salary with CPF. Worth confirming precisely.

The routes, if you ever need them

Two exist for running your own thing here, and neither is casual:

  • Employment Pass through your own company. Company incorporated more than six months, a qualifying fixed monthly salary that the company actually pays you, and commercial justification. You cannot be appointed director until the pass is approved, so incorporation usually runs through a nominee director.
  • EntrePass. At least 30% of shares, an active operating role, and one of the innovation criteria — for example S$100,000 raised from a government-recognised VC. It is built for venture-backed and IP-owning startups, which a consultancy generally is not. Realistically not your route.

Do this week, it is one hour

Find your In-Principle Approval or pass card and read who the sponsor is. Then, if there is any ambiguity at all, put the question to a corporate services firm — the ones that do this daily will answer a scoping question cheaply, and MOM's own enquiry line is free. Do not build an October strategy on an assumption about your own visa.

Nothing on this page is legal advice, and immigration rules move. Confirm before you act.

The walk

“I love love love going for walks. Listen to a podcast, be with my thoughts, stumble across new places.”

That is the only thing in this entire conversation you have described with desire rather than obligation. Everything else came out as something you ought to do. So it gets treated differently from everything else on this page.

The walk stays yours

The obvious move is to turn it into a social project — join a walking group, make it productive, attach a goal. Don't. You have one activity you genuinely want, it is solo by design, and turning it into a networking opportunity would quietly ruin it. Podcasts and your own thoughts are the point, not a gap to be filled.

Protect it. Then attach only the two things that actually belong on a walk.

What belongs on the walk

Your mum. You are already trying to call her on a Wednesday morning her time and running at 30%. Walking and talking is the natural form of a long phone call, and it means the call is not a separate appointment you have to summon — it is the walk you were going to take anyway. 14:30 SGT is roughly 07:00 in the UK. Kettle on, nobody dressed, both of you moving.

New places. Stumbling across them is the thing you like, so this needs no system. If you ever want a spine for it, the CDC heritage trails and the National Heritage Board neighbourhood routes exist and are free — five new ones launched this year, Kampong Glam, Bedok's former coastline, a Woodlands coastal route. But a direction is not a to-do list. Do not turn this into one.

Why the mum call is at 30% and how to get it higher

It is failing for exactly one reason: it is your intention, and nobody is waiting. Same failure as Duolingo, same failure as the calendar blocks.

The fix is one sentence to her: “Mum, I'm going to call you every Wednesday at seven your time.” That converts it from something you remember into something she expects, and her expectation is not yours to cancel. It is the cheapest available version of the thing that makes work stick.

And lower the bar: if you are late, call anyway. Ten minutes at 15:20 beats a clean miss. The thing that kills a weekly call is deciding you have already blown it.

The morning block

Here is the contradiction, stated plainly so it does not sit under the page unspoken: you came to me because your problem is life outside work, and I am proposing you put work into your free morning.

Four reasons it is the right call anyway.

  • It is the only thing that has ever stuck. You have two years of evidence that work survives and everything else fades. Putting Strait Up Growth in the morning is not optimism, it is playing to the one strength you have actually demonstrated.
  • It is the only item that solves four problems at once — money, the pass, the Plan B if October goes badly, and the 2027 goal of work beyond NEXT.
  • It is yours. Every hour of it buys independence from the shift that is causing all of this. That is the opposite of more work; it is the exit from this particular work.
  • Two mornings. Not five. Named days, hard stop, and it never touches Saturday or the Wednesday call.

The gate, again

If NEXT sponsors your pass, you cannot take client fees for this while that pass is live without MOM consent. What you can do in the meantime is everything that is not paid work in Singapore: sharpen the offer, build the positioning, have conversations, line up a pipeline.

And there is a third thing worth putting on the October table, after the hours and separately from the money: ask NEXT for consent to take outside consulting work. It costs them almost nothing, it is a normal request, and it is the single change that turns your Plan B from illegal to available. Ask for it last, once the hours are agreed.

The guardrail. The failure mode is obvious: this eats the whole morning and becomes the place you hide from the harder problem, which is that you know nobody here. If the morning block starts expanding past two days, it is doing the wrong job.

Health, without a health plan

Posture gone, eating worse, not cooking any more. None of that is a willpower problem either — look at the mechanism. You finish at 23:00, so dinner is either at your desk mid-shift or at half past eleven. Hawker food is three minutes away and costs nothing. Cooking for one, on a schedule that does not match your partner's, is a hard sell at any hour you are actually free.

The one move that fits your actual day

Cook in the morning. You are free from 08:00 to 16:00, the one slot almost nobody has. Make the evening meal at ten in the morning and it is sitting there at eight, ready, when you get twenty minutes between calls. It is the only version of cooking that survives your shift, and it works precisely because it is not competing with anything.

Start with the two mornings you are already using for the block. Not seven. Not a meal plan.

Posture: seven hours at a desk and almost no movement is the whole story, and the walk already does most of the repair. Do not build a programme for it. If it still bothers you in three months, see a physio once — that is an appointment someone else keeps, which is the kind that works on you.

The year map

You named this yourself as part of August 2027: a clear idea of what my year looks like. When I'll be in the UK. When I'll be travelling for work. When I'll be travelling with my partner for leisure or joining him when he's travelling for work.

And you already have a shared calendar with him, which you said is proof you both value it — it is just not being used well. That is the most promising piece of infrastructure in this entire conversation, because it is already shared, which means it already has the property that makes things stick: another person can see it.

Why it is not working, and what would

A shared calendar between two people who both struggle with executive function fails if it needs daily maintenance. So the year map has to be coarse and pull-based: months, not days; five categories, not twenty; something you both glance at rather than something either of you has to feed.

Five things belong on it: UK trips, your work travel, his work travel (which is an opportunity, not an obstacle — you can work from anywhere, so joining him is free travel with company), leisure together, and the handful of fixed anchors: Saturday, Wednesday, the two morning blocks.

Flying west trades morning for evening, hour for hour, which the app already models. Bangkok makes your evening three hours long. Dubai makes it four. That turns his work trips into a real question with a real answer, rather than a vague sense that you should travel more.

This is the one piece of software on the page I think is clearly worth building, and it is the only thing I would build next. Say the word and it gets built; if you would rather it stayed a page in a notebook, that works too.

The ADHD question

In Singapore only clinical psychologists and psychiatrists can diagnose. Private needs no referral and usually takes one to four weeks: initial consultation around S$200–400, full assessment S$1,500–2,500, evaluations of three to five hours against DSM-5 criteria. The subsidised public route is roughly S$40–60 with a substantially longer wait.

The part that does not depend on the answer

Whether or not it is ADHD, the conclusion is the same one your own history already gives you: external structure beats internal resolve. You have proved it — work stuck, everything self-directed faded. The mechanisms people lean on, body doubling and fixed appointments and someone who notices an empty chair, all work by moving the activation outside your own head. The direct evidence for body doubling specifically is thin, surveys rather than trials, but it points the same way as everything else here.

So do not make an assessment a prerequisite. It is a separate track, worth doing on its own merits, and Saturday at 07:30 does not wait for it. One note on your partner being ADHD too: it is why the year map has to be coarse. Two people who both find maintenance costly need a calendar they glance at, not one they feed.
