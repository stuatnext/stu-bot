Research on expatriate adjustment puts social integration at roughly twelve to eighteen months, and that is for people whose evenings belong to them. You are at month two with your evenings sold to London. There is no version of the last eight weeks in which you would already have a routine and a community. Where you are is the expected output, not a failure of will.

What I got wrong before this

I built you a library of 178 ideas, a directory of 608 coffee places, four views and a habit tracker. Wrong shape. A menu of 178 options is a decision you have to make every single day, which is exactly the thing you are worst at when you are tired and eight weeks off a plane. You do not need more choices. You need one appointment with other people in it.

Everything below is built to that rule, and it is deliberately small.

The hours you actually have

Work is fixed to London at 08:00–15:00 UTC. In Singapore that is 16:00–23:00. So your week is not short of time — it is short of time when other people are free.

What that closes

Nearly all of the ordinary machinery of adult friendship in a new city runs between 19:00 and 22:00 on a weekday. Sports leagues, evening classes, language exchanges, club nights, the third drink. Every generic piece of expat advice assumes you have that window. You do not, five days a week, until January. It is not that you have failed to join things. The things are shut when you are free.

What it opens

The mirror image is genuinely valuable and almost nobody has it: you are free from 8am to 4pm on a weekday. That window contains volunteering shifts that struggle to fill, daytime language classes, off-peak gyms at half price, guided nature walks, coworking floors full of other remote workers, and quiet versions of everywhere that is rammed at the weekend. Plus both weekend days, entirely.

Two fixes, both cheap

1. Stop treating calls as events. A scheduled Sunday call is a performance and it gets cancelled. Put a standing 15:00 SGT slot on one or two weekdays — their 07:00, kettle on, nobody dressed — and call it nothing. Ordinary and often beats long and scheduled.

2. The January change fixes this too. Finishing at 19:00 SGT on your Singapore days puts you free at 12:00 UK — their lunchtime. That is a second usable window, on the days that currently have none.

October: ask for the right thing

You have this planned already, so this is only about the shape of the ask. There is an arithmetic problem in it that someone will find if you do not find it first.

Do not ask for 9-to-5 Singapore. That is 01:00–09:00 UTC. It overlaps London's working day by roughly nothing, and they would be right to refuse it. And the US and Colombia precedent does not transfer, because those people sit west of London: their normal local day naturally overlaps London's afternoon by about three hours. Yours overlaps by zero. It is a genuinely different ask and it needs a genuinely different answer.

Here is the answer. Every hour later you start in Singapore buys London an hour of morning overlap and costs you an hour of your evening.

The proposal in one line

Two days a week on 12:00–19:00 SGT. London gets two hours of your morning overlap — enough for a stand-up and one meeting — and you are free at seven, which catches almost every 19:30 start in this city.

Note what that actually is: four hours of morning and four hours of evening, instead of eight and none. On those two days you would be living the Dubai shape without leaving Singapore. It is the same trade the app models, applied to your contract instead of a flight.

Three things to settle before the room

Split
Do not bundle the pay rise with the hours. Hours cost them nothing, money costs them money. Bundled, the obvious move is to pay you and defer the hours — and you would take the win and still be working at 23:00. Put the working pattern first as a low-cost operational change; let money be its own conversation a few weeks later.
Days
Monday has a problem. The all-company Weekly sits at 20:30 SGT every Monday, which is outside a 19:00 finish. Either take Tuesday and Friday, or take Monday and Friday and name the Weekly as the one standing exception. Decide before someone else raises it.
Cover
“Still available if urgent” is how these dissolve. Everything becomes urgent. Say what happens instead: which channel, what response time, who covers. A pattern with rules survives; a pattern with goodwill does not.

Free, weekly, timed 5km. Five events in Singapore: Bishan-Ang Mo Kio, East Coast Park, West Coast Park, Bay East Garden, Bedok Reservoir. Register once at parkrun.sg, print the barcode, done forever. Walking is allowed and nobody finishes last.

Go to the same one. The point is the faces, not the variety.

What to cut

You listed six things: Mandarin, new restaurants, new coffee places, activities in Singapore, building a community, volunteering. Six is a list, not a plan. Here is what I would do with it.

Keep
Community. It is the actual deficit — you said it plainly: no connections beyond your partner. But it is not a project you execute. It is the residue of turning up to the same thing repeatedly. You do not build a community; you attend something until one forms around you.
Keep
Mandarin — demoted to a vehicle. The class is the point; the language is the excuse. If you finish ten weeks having learned very little but knowing five people's names, it worked.
Demote
Restaurants, cafes, activities. These are not goals, they are what you do with people once you have them. Making them their own projects is how a social deficit gets quietly reclassified as a consumption list — and a consumption list can be completed alone forever without anything changing. The coffee directory is there for when you need somewhere to take someone. It is not a to-do list.

Volunteering — until January, unless you swap it in as the Step 4 anchor instead of Mandarin. Willing Hearts is arguably the better first anchor precisely because it has no sign-up friction at all. But one at a time. Not both.

The ADHD question

You raised it, so here is what is actually involved rather than a shrug.

In Singapore only clinical psychologists and psychiatrists can diagnose. Going private needs no referral and typically takes one to four weeks: an initial consultation runs about S$200–400 and a full assessment S$1,500–2,500, with evaluations of three to five hours using DSM-5 criteria and standardised tools. The subsidised public route is roughly S$40–60 with a substantially longer wait.

The part that does not depend on the answer

Whether or not it turns out to be ADHD, the design conclusion is identical: external structure beats internal resolve. The mechanisms people lean on — body doubling, fixed appointments, someone who notices an empty chair — work by moving the activation and monitoring outside your own head. The direct evidence for body doubling specifically is thin, surveys and clinical consensus rather than trials, but it is low-risk and it points the same way as everything else on this page.

So do not make the assessment a prerequisite for starting. It is a separate track, worth pursuing on its own merits, and Saturday at 07:30 does not wait for it.
