The rule everything follows from

Anything that looks like a job will stick. Anything that looks like a personal goal will fade. You have two years of evidence for the first half and a lifetime for the second.

Hokkien, Mandarin and English

You told me the answer, and it splits into two different problems.

Mandarin is the teachable one. Classes everywhere, daytime slots that fit your free window, roughly S$421 for ten weeks in a group of three to six. It is useful across the whole country, not just at one dinner table, and it is what younger family will switch to. Start here.

Hokkien is the one that actually gets you the elders, and it is much harder to come by — the Speak Mandarin Campaign spent forty years discouraging it, so it is rarely taught and mostly lives with older generations. Real options do exist: the Singapore Hokkien Huay Kuan Cultural Academy runs a basic conversational course, LearnDialect.sg and inlingua both teach it, and private tutors go from about S$40 an hour.

You want to do free teaching, or take an intern at Strait Up Growth who could help with your workload while learning about B2B events and media. I think this is the best idea you have had in this entire conversation, and I want to be specific about why.

It is the only thing on your list with every property that works on you

Fixed
A class or a session happens at a time somebody else set.
Expected
People turn up to be taught. They notice if you do not.
A role
You are the one who knows things. That is an identity, not a hobby.
No decision
Once it is scheduled there is nothing to choose each week.

It is, in other words, the closest thing to a job that is not one — and a job is the only category of thing you have ever sustained. Mandarin classes, parkrun and games nights all borrow one or two of those properties. This has all four.

It also solves things that have nothing to do with generosity, and it is worth being honest that they point the same way:

  • An intern is capacity. Your workload is the constraint on finding a second client, and the second client is what stops your visa depending on one contract.
  • It puts a Singaporean in your company. A one-person foreign-owned company is a thin thing on paper. A company that employs and trains locals is not.
  • Contribution to Singaporean society is part of how permanent residence gets assessed. You want to give something back and you need a PR case. Those are the same activity.

What I could not check

I ran out of research budget partway through this, so treat the last two points as the right question to ask rather than a finding. Specifically worth confirming with an immigration or corporate services specialist: how much a local hire actually moves the needle on Employment Pass renewal for a company your size, and how volunteering and community contribution are weighed in a PTS application. I believe both help. I have not verified either, and you should not plan around my belief.

There is also a practical gate: interns in Singapore generally have to be paid unless the placement comes through a structured attachment from a local polytechnic, ITE or university. Going through an institution is probably the easier route anyway — they send you candidates, the paperwork is theirs, and it costs less. Ask the corporate services firm when you get quotes.

The free teaching version is available sooner and costs nothing. Polytechnics and ITE take industry guest speakers, universities run mentoring schemes, and there are youth organisations that would take a Commercial Director who wanted to talk about how B2B media actually works. One session is not a commitment. It is a way of finding out whether you like it before you build anything around it.

Health, without a health plan

You order food because it is convenient and affordable. You do not go to hawker centres although you would like to. You drink coffee with sugar, you get occasional headaches, your posture has gone and you feel flabby. Every one of those has a mechanism rather than a moral.

The one move: hawker lunch, not cooking

I suggested cooking in the morning. Here is a better version, because it is the thing you already say you want.

Hawker centres are open in the daytime, and the daytime is precisely when you are free. So a weekday hawker lunch is cheaper than delivery, better than delivery, gets you out of the flat, and gets you out of Tanjong Pagar if you go to one somewhere else. And it attaches to the walk. Walk somewhere, eat there, walk back. No new habit — a destination for one you already have.

Cooking still helps in the evening, and the morning is still when to do it. But start with lunch, because you already want to and it requires nothing new.

Which is another argument for what is already on this page: activity-based, daytime, sober-by-default. parkrun, a class, a games night, a walk, a hawker lunch. All of those work perfectly without a drink in your hand, and none of them require you to explain yourself.

Sheffield

“My closest friends are in Sheffield. Rarely speak.” With a sad face, which I noticed.

That is the same failure as the Wednesday call: it is your intention and nobody is waiting. Friends are harder than family though, because you cannot schedule a weekly call with a mate without it being strange. So do not schedule a call. Schedule a thing.

  • A shared activity beats a catch-up. Something to do together, badly, on a schedule — a game, a fantasy league, a film you both watch and argue about. The point is that it recurs without either of you having to be the one who reaches out.
  • A group of three beats a one-to-one. Two people fall out of touch when one gets busy. Three keeps going.
  • And parkrun, again. Nine on a Saturday in Sheffield is a thing you can propose without it being A Big Reconnection Conversation.

Which is what the console is actually for

You want to buy GTA VI for escapism. Take that seriously — you do not drink, you work a punishing shift, and you need somewhere for your head to go that is not work and not worrying. A games console is a perfectly good answer and it needs no defending.

But it is worth more than escapism. It is the shared recurring activity for Sheffield and for Germany. Everything above says a catch-up call between men who have not spoken in a while is awkward and therefore does not happen, while a thing you do together does. A console plus one game everybody has is exactly that: no agenda, no reaching out, no performance of being fine. You just turn up and talk over it, which is how most long friendships actually survive distance.

One caution and then I will leave it. You finish at 23:00 and you said you sleep okay. A console is the easiest thing in the world to still be holding at two in the morning, and the sleep is load bearing for everything else here. Escapism with someone at nine on a Sunday is a different thing from escapism alone at one on a Wednesday.

It also settles the shape of the UK trips. Fewer and longer, aimed at Sheffield, beats scattered weeks that keep the tax problem alive and give you nothing.

The year map

You named this yourself as part of August 2027: a clear idea of what my year looks like. When I'll be in the UK. When I'll be travelling for work. When I'll be travelling with my partner for leisure or joining him when he's travelling for work.

And you already have a shared calendar with him, which you said is proof you both value it — it is just not being used well. That is the most promising piece of infrastructure in this entire conversation, because it is already shared, which means it already has the property that makes things stick: another person can see it.

Why it is not working, and what would

A shared calendar between two people who both struggle with executive function fails if it needs daily maintenance. So the year map has to be coarse and pull-based: months, not days; five categories, not twenty; something you both glance at rather than something either of you has to feed.

Five things belong on it: UK trips, your work travel, his work travel (which is an opportunity, not an obstacle — you can work from anywhere, so joining him is free travel with company), leisure together, and the handful of fixed anchors: Saturday, Wednesday, the two morning blocks.

Flying west trades morning for evening, hour for hour, which the app already models. Bangkok makes your evening three hours long. Dubai makes it four. That turns his work trips into a real question with a real answer, rather than a vague sense that you should travel more.

And it now has an obvious first entry. Your mum and your brother, in a named month. Put that on the map and everything else arranges itself around it: which leave you book, which months you are in the UK, which of his trips you join, when the Singapore days matter most. A year map with nothing on it is admin. A year map with your mother's visit at the top of it is a plan.

This is the one piece of software on the page I think is worth building, and the only thing I would build next. Say the word; if you would rather it stayed a page in a notebook, that genuinely works too, and the notebook version is better than a good app you do not open.

The memory thing

You have a specific learning difficulty affecting short-term memory. That is not a suspicion, it is a diagnosis, and it reframes a lot of this document.

Stop trying to remember things

Look back at everything that has failed. Duolingo needed you to remember. Calendar blocks needed you to remember what you meant when you wrote them. Streaks needed you to hold a chain in mind. The Wednesday call needs you to remember it is Wednesday. Every single one of them was a working-memory task, and you have documentation that working memory is exactly where your difficulty is.

That is not a metaphor for a character flaw. It is a processing difference with a report attached. So the rule stops being a preference and becomes an accommodation: if a thing depends on you holding it in your head, it will fail, and that is not your fault. Externalise all of it. Your mum expecting the call. A teacher noticing an empty chair. A firm filing your returns. A standing order moving the money. Nothing that lives only in your memory.

It also makes an ADHD assessment a more sensible thing to pursue rather than less. Working-memory difficulties and ADHD overlap substantially, you already have a diagnostic history to build on, and an assessor with your existing report in front of them is starting from somewhere rather than from scratch.

In Singapore only clinical psychologists and psychiatrists can diagnose. Private needs no referral and usually takes one to four weeks: consultation around S$200–400, full assessment S$1,500–2,500, three to five hours against DSM-5 criteria. The subsidised route is roughly S$40–60 with a considerably longer wait. Given money is tight, this is a genuine “later” — but bring your SpLD report when you go, and note that a diagnosis can matter for workplace adjustments if you are ever employed here rather than contracting.

Two footnotes. Your partner is ADHD, which is why the year map has to be coarse: two people who both find maintenance costly need a calendar they glance at, not one they feed. And the direct evidence for body doubling specifically is thin — surveys and clinical consensus rather than trials — but it is low-risk and points the same way as everything else here.
