One Client

You asked about life admin. Since then you have told me your partner is Singaporean and you are here indefinitely, and that turns this from a housekeeping page into the most important one of the three.

There is no second route

Your partner is Singaporean. You are here indefinitely. And Singapore does not recognise same-sex partnerships or same-sex marriages performed abroad, so there is no Dependant's Pass and no Long-Term Visit Pass available to you through him. ICA's position is that a same-sex partner applies on their own merit, and that is it.

Follow the chain

NEXT's contract funds Strait Up Growth. Strait Up Growth pays your salary. The salary holds your Employment Pass. The Employment Pass is your permission to be in the same country as your partner.

Four links, one client, and no fallback. If the contract ends, nothing catches you — not your relationship, not the fact that you live here, not that you have a company. Most people in a long-term relationship abroad have a partner-based pass sitting underneath their work pass as a floor. You do not have a floor.

I am not raising this to alarm you and I have no idea how much of it you already knew. I am raising it because every other decision on this page — the second client, the insurance, the filings, whether to pay a firm — reads completely differently once you see that your work pass is load-bearing for your relationship. And because it is worth confirming with a Singapore immigration specialist rather than taking from me.

So the real goal is PR

Permanent residence is the one thing that severs the chain. A PR does not need an employer, a sponsor or a qualifying salary to stay. It would make everything else on this page optional rather than existential.

Which means the admin is not admin

Everything boring on this page is the evidence file for that application. Consistent salary paid month after month. Tax returns filed on time, personal and corporate. A company with genuine commercial substance rather than a single invoice on repeat. Two clean years of all of it.

You are not doing compliance. You are building a case. That is a much better reason to keep it tidy, and by this project's own logic it is the right kind of reason — it has a deadline and a decision-maker who is not you.

One customer

The concentration problem was already real. It is sharper now.

A client can end a contract far more easily than an employer can end employment — you have no notice protection and no redundancy, which you traded for the structure and probably never priced. And at EP renewal, MOM looks at whether the sponsoring company is a genuine operating business. One customer and one employee is thin on paper.

The good news, and it is real

Your pass is sponsored by your own company, not by NEXT. So the secondary-employment rule that binds most EP holders does not bind you: Strait Up Growth can take as many clients as it can win, today, with nobody's consent. No MOM application, no conversation with NEXT.

Finding them is a sales problem, which is the one you are professionally qualified for. A second client thickens the PR case, de-risks the pass, and stops your right to live with your partner depending on one company's budget cycle. It is the highest-value thing in the morning block by a distance.

October is a commercial negotiation

You have said you want both the rate and the hours. Good — and as a supplier that is one conversation, not two.

You do not have an employer. You have a client, and you are their supplier. Suppliers do not request permission for their working pattern; they define a service and a price, and the client buys it or does not. Write it as a statement of work, not as a request: scope, coverage hours, response times, what is out of scope, rate.

That reverses what I told you earlier about splitting the rate from the hours. Splitting them was employee logic. In a supplier renegotiation scope and price belong in the same document, and putting them together is the stronger move, not the weaker one. You negotiate contracts for a living; you had been treating the most important negotiation of your year as an HR conversation.

The arithmetic is in the other document and it is the substance of the coverage clause: two hours of London overlap at a 19:00 Singapore finish, three at 20:00.

One caution, given everything above. You said you want to be at NEXT if it works for your lifestyle, which is exactly the right posture — but until there is a second client, walking away is not a move that is available to you. Negotiate like someone who wants a long relationship, because right now you need one.

Your friend is carrying your compliance

Worth understanding properly

Your Singaporean friend is the nominated director, and in Singapore directors are personally liable for the company's filing failures. If Strait Up Growth misses an ACRA or IRAS deadline, the fines and, at the far end, the summons or disqualification go to the director. That is him, not you.

Firms that provide nominee directors do it with an indemnity agreement and a security deposit, precisely because of this. A friend doing you a favour almost certainly has neither.

He is also, incidentally, part of your PR story: a Singaporean who vouched for you enough to put his name on your company. Do not repay that with a late filing.

The filing calendar

Everything hangs off your financial year end, which you do not currently know. Find it first — it is on your ACRA BizFile company profile, and for a new company the first FYE must fall within 18 months of incorporation.

The one you are missing

Skills Development Levy. Everyone assumes it is a local-employee thing. It is the reverse of CPF: SDL is payable on everyone on payroll including Employment Pass holders, while CPF is not. You pay yourself a salary, so the company owes it.

0.25% of monthly pay, floor S$2, cap S$11.25 a month, due by the 14th of the following month. About S$135 a year — trivial money, real compliance item, which is the worst combination because nobody notices until someone looks. Director's fees alone attract no SDL; a director on a monthly salary does. You are the second.

Small piece of good news. A new Singapore company gets a start-up exemption for its first three years of assessment: 75% off the first S$100,000 of chargeable income and 50% off the next S$100,000. Worth knowing before deciding how much to take as salary versus leave as profit — though note that for PR purposes, a consistent and respectable salary is the number that gets looked at.

Insurance: three gaps, one urgent

Urgent
Health. NEXT's cover does not extend, you hold an EP, and you have nothing here. Singapore's healthcare is excellent and priced accordingly, and you are one accident away from a bill with no policy behind it. Largest practical exposure in your life and the cheapest to close.
Check
Professional indemnity, territorially. Your indemnity cover is a UK policy. Most carry territorial and jurisdiction limits, and the work is now delivered by a Singapore company. Ask your insurer in one email: does this respond to work performed in Singapore by a Singapore entity, for clients outside the UK? If not, you are uninsured the moment the second client signs — and PI is the first thing a serious client asks to see.
Consider
Income protection. One-person company: no work means no revenue, no salary, and eventually no pass. Your UK critical injury cover is a lump sum for catastrophe, not income replacement for six months of being unwell — and six months of being unwell is the scenario that actually threatens the chain at the top of this page.

The UK

The property

If you are letting it, you are a non-resident landlord the moment you are out of the UK for six months, whatever your nationality. Two consequences:

  • File NRL1. Without it your agent or tenant must withhold basic-rate tax from the rent and hand it to HMRC. With it you receive rent gross and settle through Self Assessment. Approval takes about 90 days, so it is a now job, not a later one.
  • Tell your lender. Most UK mortgages require consent to let and notification if you move abroad. Letting without it is a breach of the mortgage terms.

UK rental income stays UK-taxable regardless of where you live, and you will need to file Self Assessment annually. The consolation is that Singapore does not tax it — foreign-sourced income received by individuals here is generally exempt, so there is no second bite.

And for whenever you sell: as a non-resident you must report and pay CGT within 60 days of completion. It is the single most commonly missed UK deadline for people in your position and it carries penalties.

The ISA, and the answer you did not want

You cannot contribute any more

You said you would really like to keep paying into your stocks and shares ISA. You cannot — non-UK residents are not permitted to subscribe to an ISA, and there is no workaround. What you already hold stays open and keeps its UK tax treatment; it simply stops taking new money.

The genuine consolation is that you may end up better off. Singapore has no capital gains tax, and foreign dividends received here by an individual are generally exempt. An ordinary Singapore brokerage account is, for you, close to an unlimited ISA.

One structural trap worth knowing before you open one. Singapore has no tax treaty with the US, so:

The pension

You can generally still pay up to £3,600 gross a year for five tax years after the year you left, with tax relief at source, if you had relevant UK earnings in the year of departure. Check whether that applies to you — it is a small window and it closes.

Nothing is saving for you any more

Look at what has quietly happened. As an EP holder you are outside CPF, so no employer or employee contributions. You are no longer auto-enrolled in a UK pension. You cannot contribute to your ISA. Your retirement provision is now zero unless you personally make it happen every month.

Which, given the finding that self-directed things fade on you, means it must not be a monthly decision. Set up a standing instruction on payday and never think about it again. Automation is the version of discipline that has ever worked for you.
