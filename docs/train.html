You cannot target belly fat. Nobody can.

This is the part I am not going to soften, because you will otherwise spend six months doing sit-ups and get nothing for it. Spot reduction is not a thing. There is no exercise, no routine and no supplement that takes fat off one named area. Fat comes off the whole body in an order your genetics chose, and for most men the abdomen and flanks are close to last. Doing a thousand crunches trains the muscle underneath and leaves the layer above it exactly where it was.

So what actually changes how that area looks?

Muscle
Broader shoulders and a thicker upper back make the same waist look narrower. This is the single fastest visible change available to you, and it happens in months, not years.
Posture
You sit for a seven-hour shift. Tight hip flexors tilt the pelvis forward and push the lower belly out; rounded shoulders collapse the chest. Both are trainable and both change the silhouette without a gram of fat moving. For a lot of desk-bound men this is most of the effect in the first two months.
Bracing
The deep abdominal wall is a corset. Trained properly — carries, planks, anti-rotation — it holds the whole midsection in. Crunches do not do this.
Not this
Crunches, sit-ups, ab machines, waist trainers, HIIT "belly fat burners", anything sold as targeted. None of it does what it says.

How to get stronger, written down so you do not have to remember it

Pick a weight you could do two or three more reps with than the bottom of the range. Every session, try to add one rep to each set. When you hit the top of the range on all three sets, add the smallest increment the gym has and drop back to the bottom of the range.

That is the whole method. It is called double progression and it is the only thing you need to know for the first year. Five minutes of easy cardio to warm up, then two light sets of the first exercise. Sessions run about fifty minutes.

Every slot has machine alternatives, and the app carries them

Your gym is full of machines, so nothing in the programme is a single named exercise — each slot is a job, and anything in its list does that job. Tap the swap arrows next to any exercise in the app and pick whatever is free. A busy rack is not a reason to go home.

Each variant keeps its own weight history, because a leg press and a goblet squat are not the same number and averaging them would make the progression advice nonsense. Swap to the hack squat and it starts you fresh; swap back and your dumbbell weight is still there waiting.

Machines are also easier to learn alone, need no spotter, and are far less exposed than a rack in the middle of the floor — which for the first month matters more than any theoretical advantage free weights have.

The unwritten rules, written down

Wiping
Wipe the bench or machine after you use it. There is spray and paper on the wall. This is the only rule anyone actually minds about.
Weights
Put them back on the rack, in the right place, matched pairs. Takes ten seconds and it is the whole of gym etiquette.
Sharing
If someone is on the machine you want, say "mind if I work in?" They will say yes. It means you alternate sets. This is completely normal and asking it marks you as someone who knows the room, not someone who does not.
Asking
Ask the staff where anything is, or how a machine adjusts. That is literally the job you are paying for. Nobody thinks less of it.
Being watched
You are not. Everyone there is looking at themselves, their phone, or the ceiling while they count. The self-consciousness is real and it is also completely one-directional, and it fades at about visit four.
