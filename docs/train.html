The Middle

You asked for a routine to lose belly fat and love handles. The routine is below and it is specific. But the first three sections are the ones that decide whether it works, because the thing you described is real and the fix is not the one you named.

You are not overweight

182centimetres
71.5kilograms, roughly
21.6BMI · healthy is 18.5–24.9

You are on the lean side of normal. Not borderline, not "technically fine" — comfortably inside it, with room below you. So the sentence "I definitely need to lose weight" is, as a matter of arithmetic, false.

That is not me being nice about it. It matters because it changes the entire plan. If you eat less and run more from here, you lose the small amount of muscle you have alongside the fat, your face and shoulders go first, and the soft middle stays roughly where it is — only now it is sitting on a smaller frame, which reads as worse, not better. Plenty of men have chased this exact feeling down to 65kg and arrived looking softer than when they started.

What you actually have is a shape problem, not a weight problem

Normal weight, low muscle, fat sitting where men store it first. There is a gym term for it and it is not a kind one, so ignore the term and take the mechanism: at 182cm you are carrying very little structure. There is nothing under the skin to give the chest an edge or the waist a taper, so everything reads as soft — even at a weight most people would call slim.

The fix is to add muscle, not subtract weight. Your bodyweight should go up over the next six months, and you should be pleased when it does.

You cannot target belly fat. Nobody can.

This is the part I am not going to soften, because you will otherwise spend six months doing sit-ups and get nothing for it. Spot reduction is not a thing. There is no exercise, no routine and no supplement that takes fat off one named area. Fat comes off the whole body in an order your genetics chose, and for most men the abdomen and flanks are close to last. Doing a thousand crunches trains the muscle underneath and leaves the layer above it exactly where it was.

So what actually changes how that area looks?

Muscle
Broader shoulders and a thicker upper back make the same waist look narrower. This is the single fastest visible change available to you, and it happens in months, not years.
Posture
You sit for a seven-hour shift. Tight hip flexors tilt the pelvis forward and push the lower belly out; rounded shoulders collapse the chest. Both are trainable and both change the silhouette without a gram of fat moving. For a lot of desk-bound men this is most of the effect in the first two months.
Bracing
The deep abdominal wall is a corset. Trained properly — carries, planks, anti-rotation — it holds the whole midsection in. Crunches do not do this.
Not this
Crunches, sit-ups, ab machines, waist trainers, HIIT "belly fat burners", anything sold as targeted. None of it does what it says.

The programme

Three full-body sessions a week, plus the parkrun you already have on Saturday. Full body rather than a body-part split for one reason that matters more than any other: if you miss a session, you have not missed a muscle group for the week. You told me travel and uprooting killed every routine you have had. This one is built to survive a missed Wednesday.

Put them at 09:30 on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. You wake around 08:30 and Malta does not start until 16:00, so you have a seven-and-a-half hour window that nobody else has a claim on. It is the only part of your day that cannot be taken off you, which is exactly why the thing you keep failing to do belongs in it.

How to get stronger, written down so you do not have to remember it

Pick a weight you could do two or three more reps with than the bottom of the range. Every session, try to add one rep to each set. When you hit the top of the range on all three sets, add the smallest increment the gym has and drop back to the bottom of the range.

That is the whole method. It is called double progression and it is the only thing you need to know for the first year. Five minutes of easy cardio to warm up, then two light sets of the first exercise. Sessions run about fifty minutes.

The travel version

You are in the UK on the 8th and Bulgaria on the 9th for the retreat. This is precisely the point where every previous routine of yours has died, so it gets its own plan rather than a hope.

Twenty minutes, hotel room, no equipment, on any day you cannot get to a gym:

  • Push-ups — 4 sets, stopping two reps short of failure
  • Split squats — 3 × 12 each leg
  • Single-leg Romanian deadlift — 3 × 10 each, bodyweight is enough
  • Row on a table edge or with a loaded backpack — 3 × 12
  • Side plank — 3 × 40s each side
  • Dead bug — 3 × 12 each

This is not as good as the gym and it does not need to be. It exists so the run does not break, and so you come home having not lost the thread. Airport walking counts as walking.

The coffee

You take it with sugar and you drink several a day. Two teaspoons in three coffees is roughly a hundred calories of nothing at all — no protein, no fullness, no pleasure you would miss after a fortnight. I am not going to tell you to stop drinking coffee, which would be both miserable and pointless. Halve the sugar, or move to one sweet one in the morning and unsweetened after that. It is the only dietary change in this entire document, because it is the only one worth the friction.

You do not drink alcohol. That is a genuinely large advantage here and you should know it is one.

Stop weighing yourself

The scale is going to mislead you badly on this specific plan, because the plan is designed to make the number go up. Measure these instead:

Waist
Tape measure at the navel, Sunday morning, before eating, same conditions every week. This is the number that answers your actual question. It can fall while your weight rises. That is the whole point.
Photo
Same spot, same light, same time of day, once a month. You will not see change day to day and you will see it clearly across eight weeks. Your memory of what you looked like is not reliable evidence — and you have told me your short-term memory is genuinely a problem, so do not make it do this job.
The weights
Write down what you lifted. If your dumbbell bench goes from 12kg to 22kg over three months, your chest has changed whether or not you can see it yet. This is the most honest feedback in the whole system and it arrives every single session.

What to expect, honestly

Weeks 1–3

Nothing visible, big strength jumps

Early gains are mostly your nervous system learning the movements. Add weight fast, expect the mirror to show nothing. This is the phase where people quit.

Weeks 4–8

Posture, then clothes

The first visible change is how you stand. Shirts sit differently across the shoulders before anything happens at the waist.

Months 3–6

The chest and shoulders

This is where the flatness you described actually resolves — not by losing anything, but by there finally being something there. Expect to be 2–4kg heavier and look considerably better.

Last

The waist and the flanks

They always go last, in every man, regardless of the programme. When they do start to change it will be because of everything above, not because of anything you did to them directly. If you get to month four and the belly is the only thing that has not moved, the programme is working normally.

One caveat and then I will stop

This is general training and nutrition guidance, not medical advice. You mentioned occasional headaches. If they get worse or change pattern once you start training, that is worth a GP appointment rather than a wait-and-see — and if anything hurts sharply in a joint during a lift, stop that lift rather than working through it.
